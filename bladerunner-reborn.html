The game in a few lines

This game is a 2.5D roguelite based on the Blade Runner license, where you play as a wanted replicant looking for revenge and trying to recover his lost memories, with the help of stolen weapons and body enhancements.

About the concept

Where the idea came from

We had to respect the universe of our given license. We also had constraints, like having a hub, skills and a merchant.

We took inspiration from games like Hades and Dead Cells, since we needed to create procedurally generated levels.

Concept art / reference
My process

From idea to playable level

STEP 01

Scenario

Since in Blade Runner we usually follow the point of view of an agent, I found it more interesting to switch to a Replicant’s. I preferred doing this because many of this license’s themes had already been explored.

This gave me more freedom and room for a new story, and to show a new side of Los Angeles.

Scenario / storyboard
STEP 02

Los Angeles

The city is plunged into deep darkness after a huge blackout drained it of its lifeblood. During my research on the license, I found this event pretty interesting.

It allowed me to set up a story around the sensation of being a fugitive in an everyday environment that is now the worst enemy of its inhabitants.

I wanted the main character to feel like this while seeking revenge: even though he’s a replicant, that doesn’t make him immune to a manhunt.

Paper map / top-down layout
STEP 03

Storytelling

To tell my story, I conceived each chapter like a fresh start.

Each time a chapter ends with the death of an antagonist, the next one brings new information about the main character, which also echoes the lore of the world, while guiding the player to his next objective.

Beat chart / flow diagram
STEP 04

Emotional arc

To emphasize the fact that the main character is being chased, I thought that managing tension was important for the right experience.

I wanted the player, after a boss fight or after learning big pieces of the story, to have time to absorb and analyze them, instead of feeling rushed, which would shift the player’s attention elsewhere.

The challenges

This time I had to adapt my narration very often depending on my team’s needs. I worked a lot with the character artist and level designers, who had different needs that weren’t easy to fulfill at the same time.

It was also my first time as a lead, and it taught me how to better manage my time, help others and keep everyone aligned with our core intentions.

Problems encountered

We had some communication problems in our team that later turned into delays and sudden issues that slowed us down.

There was also a loss of motivation and conflicts that needed to be resolved quickly and peacefully.
