Pitch

The game in a few lines

Pestilence is an action-adventure game in a medieval setting, where an alchemist has to find a cure for the Plague, using alchemy notions found in ancient books.

Trailer / gameplay video
About the concept

Where the idea came from

To do this game, I needed to create a new language, so I took inspiration from dead languages, like dead Germanic dialects from the 13th century, one especially: Greenlandic Norse.

Its alphabet is composed of runes, used by the Norse settlers in Greenland. So, I decided to take this language and mix it with alchemy.

My process

From idea to playable level

STEP 01

Conception

I wanted to create a world with a dark secret to unveil, where the player would have exactly the same knowledge of it as the character, to increase the immersion.

I wanted the world to look beautiful while also showing the gruesome atmosphere that the Plague created, and how people lose their humanity under certain circumstances.

I also thought that it would be interesting to have a skill tree where you need to find the right combination of runes to unlock new recipes. This reinforces the immersion in the role of an alchemist apprentice who's discovering lost knowledge.

1 / 7
STEP 02

Layout

I designed a village of farmers and merchants that has been attacked by the Plague and the Royal Guard.

The focus of the level is stealth. I wanted to make the player feel like a prey while exploring the narrow streets of the village overwhelmed by rats. This allowed me to play with tension and create situations of stress to reinforce the “prey” aspect.

However, I didn't want to limit the player to the stealth approach: it's also possible to fight the guards, but at a high cost in terms of resources.

1 / 5
STEP 03

Narrative overview

I tell my story through environmental narration, but also with barks from the guards. The main goal is to make the player feel like the hero who has to save humanity from collapsing under the Plague.

Starting with little information and gathering it along the way increases the feeling of being the only hope for salvation. That's also why most of the main character's journey is solitary, like a beam of light surrounded by darkness.

So, the player feels the weight and the pressure of this world on his shoulders too.

1 / 3
STEP 04

Blockout & iterations

I wanted to alternate exterior and interior sections of the village, but I didn't want to give the feeling of total safety while there.

So in these kinds of zones I used obstacles like miasma or rats to keep the player on his toes.

I also needed to guide the player through these events without creating confusion or misunderstandings on the correct path. The solution was to use items like furniture, boxes or carts to create leading lines that give a direction, also reinforced by the lights of torches.

1 / 2
STEP 05

Final level

The village had to feel alive even without the player. To do so, I had to think about what happened before his arrival.

In zones like the market, the deposit and the alchemist's house, I arranged the placement of items and furniture to hint that there was a fight, someone fleeing or being captured in the rush.

The challenges

I found it pretty difficult to conceive the level for a stealth approach while also leaving the player free to choose another strategy.

This exercise made me more conscious about giving player freedom the same importance as the storytelling.

Problems encountered

The size of the map was a problem I noticed when I started to fill the spaces with elements. Streets were too large, some sections too long, causing an unwanted drop in tension.

However, after some changes, spaces started to feel less empty and useless, but this correction cost me some precious time.
